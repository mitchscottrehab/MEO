The way out

Stop asking "does it hurt?" Start asking "what can it handle, and how do I know?"

Move 01, sixty seconds

Where are you, really?

Feeling good is a terrible test for being ready. Answer these instead. Can you...

1Get through a normal day without thinking about it?
2Still train most of your week, around it?
3Load it heavy (roughly a 10-rep max) in the last two weeks?
4Do sprints, jumps or intervals, and feel fine the next morning?
5Do a full normal session (race pace, top sets, your game) and feel fine the next morning?

Move 03, your next session

Turn one dial. Leave the rest.

Rest is not rehab. Before you drop a session, change one thing about it.

Load

Same lift, lighter.

Range

Shorter depth or stride.

Speed

Slower reps, easier pace.

Volume

Fewer sets, kms or minutes.

How often

Every second day.

Where most runners start: speed. Easy runs only. Park the intervals.

One dial at a time. Judge it tomorrow morning. Everything it doesn't bother, train hard.

Train the good side hard too. Some of that strength carries over to the injured side.

Move 04, tomorrow, before you get up

Judge it by tomorrow morning.

During tells you very little. The morning after tells you what you need to know.

Green

Settled by morning, the week trending flat.

Keep going. Next week, turn one dial back up.

Orange

Still there in the morning, or creeping up over the week.

Turn one dial down. Don't stop everything.

Red

Severe, getting worse fast, or something new.

Stop that activity and get seen.

Score it out of 10 first thing in the morning. Worse than the day before? There's your first alert. Staying below a 3 or 4 out of 10 and not getting worse is the first goal.

A starting point, not a prescription. Agree your own pain rule and progression with your clinician.

The thing nobody tells you

Low load gets low results.

If you could do thirty reps, it's too light. Strength adaptations are driven largely by how hard each set is, and the best everyday measure of that is how close to failure you finish.

How many good reps did you have left when you stopped the set?

8

Go heavier.

Stop with that many easy reps left and your body gets very little signal to change anything.

Run

Calf raises with a plate on, not 50 at bodyweight.

Lift

The real lift, lighter. Not a band version of it.

Play

Hops, bounds and hard stops, built up. Not just balance work.

Heavy means heavy for where you are now. Build into it over a few weeks and let tomorrow morning referee.

The one section to take seriously

When to stop reading and get seen.

If any of these apply, skip the rest of this guide and see a doctor or physio in person, today if you can.

Get help today

  • Numbness around the groin, inner thighs or backside, or new bladder or bowel problems.
  • New numbness, tingling, pins and needles, or weakness, especially if it's getting worse.
  • Severe pain that's getting worse, or pain that wakes you at night and doesn't change when you move.
  • You can't put weight on it, or it looks out of shape.
  • It started with a big fall, crash or collision, or a smaller fall if you're older or have osteoporosis.

Also get it checked first

  • A hot, red, swollen joint, especially with a fever or feeling unwell.
  • Calf pain or swelling, especially after a long flight, a long drive or surgery.
  • Fever, chills or weight loss you can't explain.
  • A history of cancer and new pain.
  • New back pain when you're under 20 or over 50, have a weakened immune system, a recent bacterial infection, or use IV drugs.
000

Chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or anything that feels like an emergency. Call now.
