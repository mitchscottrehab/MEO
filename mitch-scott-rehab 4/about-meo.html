MEO /mĕ-ō/ • Latin

1. I move. To flow, pass through, and advance continuously.

2. My own. To possess, command, and reclaim what is yours.

THE PIVOT

MEO was built for one type of person.

The person who trains before work. Who has a race on the calendar they're not willing to scratch. Who knows exactly what their body is capable of and refuses to accept that injury is the end of that story.

Not the end. The opportunity.

Because returning from injury isn't just about getting back to where you were. The goal is to come back better than you were... moving better, loading better, and more resilient than before the injury happened.

We don't just rehab the injury. We rebuild what the injury took... your capacity, your confidence, and your ability to move without second-guessing every step.

THE PROBLEM

The system wasn't built for you.

It was built for the average patient. The person who just wants less pain and is happy to rest until it settles.

That's not you.

You set a 5am alarm. You track your sessions. You've built a body, a capacity, a version of yourself, over years of showing up. Right now, it feels like it's slipping.

The appointment was 20 minutes. The exercises didn't connect to your training, your load, or what your body is actually being asked to do. And when you asked what returning to training looks like, the answer didn't come with a timeline, a target, or a reason.

That's not a plan. It's the absence of one.

THE DEEPER TRUTH

Pain-free isn't the finish line.

Here's what nobody tells you... pain doesn't mean damage. And the absence of pain doesn't mean you're ready.

Your body is always asking one question: can you handle this? Not today, moving carefully in a controlled room. At full speed. Under load. In the exact patterns your sport demands.

Most rehab stops the moment pain does. So you return to training with less capacity than you think. For movements you haven't prepared for.

So you come back. Three sessions in, it flares. Not because you're unlucky. Not because your body is broken. Because nobody built you back up to what your sport/activity actually asks of you.

You've been treating the symptom. Nobody has treated you.
